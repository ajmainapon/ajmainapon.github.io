About Me

I am a final-year Robotics & Mechatronics Engineering undergraduate at the University of Dhaka, graduating in October 2026.

My research lies at the intersection of generative models, controllable perception, and self-supervised AI. Specifically, I am interested in how structure and self-supervision can cleanly steer what a neural network learns in low-data regimes, mitigating high annotation costs.

Currently, I am conducting senior thesis research on steering Latent Diffusion Models via graph-embedded spatial guidance to improve memorability, as well as independent work on label-efficient 3D medical segmentation via volumetric self-supervised pretraining (JEPA). Previously, I curated and trained YOLOv11 pipelines for stomata micro-image detection in a funded institutional project.

Beyond research, I am a passionate problem solver, having solved 500+ competitive programming challenges in C++ on Codeforces.

Generative Memorability — Steering Latent Diffusion via Graph-Embedded Spatial Guidance
Md. Ajmain Istiak Apon, Faculty Advisor.
Senior Research Thesis, Department of Robotics & Mechatronics Engineering, University of Dhaka, 2025–2026

Integrated AMNet memorability guidance into a Latent Diffusion Model workflow, steering visual generation toward highly memorable configurations while preserving fidelity. Achieved a +60% memorability uplift across a 20-prompt evaluation set, validated using ResMem as a scoring model. Currently extending the architecture with graph-embedded spatial guidance via cross-attention adjustments and custom U-Net steering.

Label-Efficient Volumetric Medical Segmentation via Volumetric SSL Pretraining
Md. Ajmain Istiak Apon, Independent Researcher.
Preprint in preparation, 2025

Designing a label-efficient 3D segmentation pipeline using ViT-Base coupled with Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA). Targeting competitive volumetric segmentations using only 3–5 annotated scans. Implementing self-supervised pretraining on 1,200+ unannotated CT scans (TotalSegmentator) utilizing 90% slice masking to capture robust global spatial geometries under hardware-constrained gradient accumulation parameters.
