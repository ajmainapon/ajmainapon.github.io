I am a final-year undergraduate researcher at the University of Dhaka, studying Robotics & Mechatronics Engineering. I expect to complete my degree in February 2027. My research focuses on world models and LLM reasoning. I have studied how action representations affect predicted futures, why language models repeat incorrect solutions, and what independent verification can recover. For my PhD, I am interested in world models, LLM post-training and reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR). My current work studies self-correction in language models: why they anchor on their own wrong solutions, whether fine-tuned LoRA editors can help, and how to spend inference-time compute on verification instead of repair. Earlier projects include self-supervised CT pretraining (Mind the Gap), stomatal phenotyping, and diffusion guidance (GenMem). I also work on video-to-audio generation as a side project. I am seeking PhD opportunities for Fall 2027. Please get in touch if our research interests align.

Was the Model Given the Right Action? Auditing and Repairing Action Interfaces in Navigation World Models

Research project · Completed experiments; manuscript

NWM’s released rollout code encodes actions differently from its training code. Repairing the inputs cuts prediction error by 42% without retraining, and ActRosetta checks for and repairs such mismatches in other models.

Details
  • Found three interacting mismatches between NWM’s training code and its released rollout code: reference frame, normalization offset and yaw wrapping. The two encodings agree on the first prediction step, so a one-step check passes.
  • Repairing only the action inputs, with the checkpoint unchanged, reduced mean DreamSim error on RECON from 0.240 to 0.139 (42%): a 0.101 reduction, 95% CI [0.076, 0.123], over 150 clips and prediction times 1–16 s. All five public checkpoints improved on a new 120-clip cohort, and repaired predictions also followed the commanded actions more closely.
  • Repair changed which NWM checkpoint ranks best (exploratory) and removed or shrank the measured leads of EgoWM and RAE-NWM over NWM. Correcting only the normalization offset increased error, so the mismatches must be checked together.
  • Injected encoding faults also increased error in EgoWM and RAE-NWM (90 clips, five seeds). Injected faults are not bugs in those models’ released code.
  • Built ActRosetta, a tool that runs paired training and evaluation action encoders on controlled paths, names a differing field only when a test path shows it, and repairs inputs when the full encoding is determined.
  • On the final sealed test of ActionFaultBench (240 encoder pairs written by a separate automated agent after the code was frozen), ActRosetta detected all 91 differing pairs, named their differences exactly in 73 (80%, below the 95% target), refused all 48 out-of-scope pairs, and repaired all 38 differing pairs that could be repaired from outputs. An audit of 40 unexamined public repositories found five mismatches.
  • Scope: model evidence centres on NWM and RECON. Repair helps little where predictions barely respond to actions (SCAND, Go Stanford), and improved navigation control has not been demonstrated.

No Changes Needed: Why Self-Correction Repeats Wrong Math Answers, and What Independent Sampling Recovers

Research manuscript

Wrong-draft anchoring, counterfactual edits, and independent verification on Qwen and Llama models.

Details
  • Studied why a language model shown its own complete but incorrect solution often accepts and repeats the error instead of re-deriving the answer. Compared draft-conditioned repair with independent, draft-free sampling.
  • Implemented correction and preservation LoRA editors, action-relative controllers, and later verification experiments. A 506-draft counterfactual study examined whether the derivation or stated conclusion drives repetition.
  • Evaluated confidence-aware verification and adaptive sampling on Qwen2.5-Coder-7B and Llama-3.1-8B, with 200 development and 600 held-out numeric MATH prompts per model, paired comparisons, and explicit token-cost accounting.
  • Reported failed pre-specified hypotheses and null comparisons. The manuscript attributes the measured verification advantage to confidence weighting rather than a novel stopping rule; gains are modest and bounded to the tested setting.
  • Research direction: reliable reasoning, verification, and inference-time computation; motivates my broader interest in LLM post-training and RLVR.

Mind the Gap: A Controlled Evaluation of Slice Separation and Masking in Self-Supervised CT Pretraining

First-author research manuscript

A controlled study of context–target separation, masking, and label efficiency in medical CT pretraining.

Code ↗
Details
  • Built I-JEPA-CT, a 2.5D cross-slice joint-embedding predictive pretraining pipeline (ViT-B/16 context encoder, EMA target encoder, 4-layer Transformer predictor) trained on 1,082 unlabeled TotalSegmentator CT volumes for ~333k iterations on a single GPU.
  • Ran a controlled 2×2 factorial study over context–target slice gap and masking geometry across five label budgets; contrary to video-SSL intuition, narrowing the slice gap (A2) improved linear-probe Dice by 53–202% over the wide-gap baseline and dominated the masking factor, while combining both changes underperformed A2 by 12–17%, exposing a non-additive interaction.
  • Showed that the measured SSL benefit is protocol-dependent: A2 gains +102–118% over random init under linear probing but only +13.0 / +8.5 / +2.8% under end-to-end fine-tuning at N=20/50/100 (Welch t: p=0.017, p<0.001, p=0.054), with fine-tuning seed variance reduced 1.3–2.2×; A2 recovers 67% of supervised nnU-Net Dice at N=50 (liver 90%).
  • Tested robustness via cross-dataset transfer to AMOS22-CT (+158–260% over random init), CT→MR transfer with no MR pretraining (+41–48%), and a matched 3D SwinUNETR comparison that reproduces the same label-dependent decline in fine-tuning gain.
