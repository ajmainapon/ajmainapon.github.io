About Me

I am a final-year Robotics & Mechatronics Engineering undergraduate student at the University of Dhaka, Bangladesh, expecting to graduate in October 2026.

My research spans self-supervised representation learning, world models, controllable text-to-image generative models, and computer vision benchmarks. I am particularly interested in how self-supervision and training-free guidance can steer neural network representations in low-data regimes.

Currently, I am conducting senior thesis research on steering Latent Diffusion Models via predicted memorability guidance (GenMem), as well as first-author research (under review) titled Mind the Gap, a controlled evaluation of context–target slice separation and masking geometry in 2.5D joint-embedding predictive (JEPA) pretraining for label-efficient CT segmentation. Previously, I developed instance-segmentation benchmarks for stomatal phenotyping in a funded research project.

Most recently, my team NeurAps won the Insight 2.0 Datathon 2026 (Champion of 79 teams), a SEER lung-cancer survival prediction challenge organized by the IASDS Students' Club, University of Dhaka.

Senior Thesis · Active
Senior Thesis — GenMem: Auditing Predicted-Memorability Guidance in Text-to-Image Diffusion
Md. Ajmain Istiak Apon (Under Faculty Supervision)
Department of Robotics & Mechatronics Engineering, University of Dhaka | Jan 2026 – Present
  • Implemented GenMem, a training-free guidance method that differentiates a pretrained memorability predictor (AMNet) through the DDIM sampling trajectory of Stable Diffusion, leaving all model weights frozen.
  • On DrawBench (n=200): held-out memorability rose +3.42% (ResMem) and +2.72% (ViTMem), both p < 10⁻⁸, with no significant CLIP-fidelity loss; replicated on COCO (n=200) with equal or larger effect sizes.
  • Validated causality via reverse-guidance symmetry and a null random-critic control; characterized proxy over-optimization in SDXL and mapped a three-regime dose–response curve across an 8-point guidance-weight sweep.

First-Author Paper · Under Review
Mind the Gap: A Controlled Evaluation of Slice Separation and Masking in Self-Supervised CT Pretraining
Md. Ajmain Istiak Apon (First Author)
Under review (double-blind) | Jan 2026 – Sep 2026
  • Built I-JEPA-CT, a 2.5D cross-slice joint-embedding predictive pretraining pipeline (ViT-B/16 context encoder, EMA target encoder, 4-layer Transformer predictor) trained on 1,082 unlabeled TotalSegmentator CT volumes for ~333k iterations on a single GPU.
  • Ran a controlled 2×2 factorial study over context–target slice gap and masking geometry across five label budgets; contrary to video-SSL intuition, narrowing the slice gap (A2) improved linear-probe Dice by 53–202% over the wide-gap baseline and dominated the masking factor, while combining both changes underperformed A2 by 12–17%, exposing a non-additive interaction.
  • Showed that the measured SSL benefit is protocol-dependent: A2 gains +102–118% over random init under linear probing but only +13.0 / +8.5 / +2.8% under end-to-end fine-tuning at N=20/50/100 (Welch t: p=0.017, p<0.001, p=0.054), with fine-tuning seed variance reduced 1.3–2.2×; A2 recovers 67% of supervised nnU-Net Dice at N=50 (liver 90%).
  • Tested robustness via cross-dataset transfer to AMOS22-CT (+158–260% over random init), CT→MR transfer with no MR pretraining (+41–48%), and a matched 3D SwinUNETR comparison that reproduces the same label-dependent decline in fine-tuning gain.
Stack: PyTorch, timm, MONAI, Nibabel, CUDA

Insight 2.0 Datathon 2026 — Cancer Survival Classification (Team NeurAps)
Md. Ajmain Istiak Apon (Team NeurAps)
IASDS Students' Club, Institute of Applied Statistics & Data Science, University of Dhaka | 27 Aug – 5 Sep 2026
