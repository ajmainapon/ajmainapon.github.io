About Me

I am a final-year Robotics & Mechatronics Engineering undergraduate student at the University of Dhaka, Bangladesh, expecting to complete my degree in February 2027.

My research asks when learned predictions and reasoning can be trusted. I investigate action conditioning in navigation world models and why language models repeat incorrect reasoning when asked to correct themselves. My intended PhD directions are world models, RLVR, and LLM post-training.

My current projects include an empirical audit of navigation-world-model action interfaces and a study of wrong-draft anchoring, independent verification, and inference-time computation in LLMs. Earlier work includes Mind the Gap, on label-efficient CT pretraining, and my undergraduate thesis GenMem, on predicted-memorability guidance in diffusion models. I also explore physics-conditioned video-to-audio generation as a side project.

Most recently, my team NeurAps won the Insight 2.0 Datathon 2026 (Champion of 79 teams), a SEER lung-cancer survival prediction challenge organized by the IASDS Students' Club, University of Dhaka.

World Models · Completed Experiments / Manuscript

Navigation World Models: Auditing Action Interfaces

Research project · 2026 · Updated November 2026
  • Identified mismatches between training and rollout action representations in released NWM evaluation: coordinate frames, affine-normalization offsets, and yaw wrapping. Implemented corrected action construction and model-free reconstruction checks.
  • In a paired RECON evaluation (150 clips, 20 trajectories, seed 0, 250 diffusion steps), correcting the interface reduced mean DreamSim error from 0.240 to 0.139: a 0.101 reduction with trajectory-cluster 95% CI [0.076, 0.123], without retraining.
  • Completed attribution, seed and frame-rate checks, and dataset comparisons. The improvement was substantially smaller on Go Stanford and SCAND, bounding the original result.
  • Extended fault-injection experiments to EgoWM and RAE-NWM on 90 clips from 19 trajectories with three seeds and paired comparisons within GPU type. Tested faults increased perceptual error; injected faults do not establish existing bugs in those models.
  • Scope: image-prediction fidelity and action-representation sensitivity. Improved closed-loop navigation success has not been demonstrated.

LLM Reasoning · Completed Experiments / Manuscript

No Changes Needed: Why Self-Correction Repeats Wrong Math Answers, and What Independent Sampling Recovers

Research project · 2026 · Updated November 2026
  • Studied why a language model shown its own complete but incorrect solution often accepts and repeats the error instead of re-deriving the answer. Compared draft-conditioned repair with independent, draft-free sampling.
  • Implemented correction and preservation LoRA editors, action-relative controllers, and later verification experiments. A 506-draft counterfactual study examined whether the derivation or stated conclusion drives repetition.
  • Evaluated confidence-aware verification and adaptive sampling on Qwen2.5-Coder-7B and Llama-3.1-8B, with 200 development and 600 held-out numeric MATH prompts per model, paired comparisons, and explicit token-cost accounting.
  • Reported failed pre-specified hypotheses and null comparisons. The manuscript attributes the measured verification advantage to confidence weighting rather than a novel stopping rule; gains are modest and bounded to the tested setting.
  • Research direction: reliable reasoning, verification, and inference-time computation; motivates my broader interest in LLM post-training and RLVR.

Senior Thesis · Active
Senior Thesis — GenMem: Auditing Predicted-Memorability Guidance in Text-to-Image Diffusion
Md. Ajmain Istiak Apon (Under Faculty Supervision)
Department of Robotics & Mechatronics Engineering, University of Dhaka | Jan 2026 – Present
  • Implemented GenMem, a training-free guidance method that differentiates a pretrained memorability predictor (AMNet) through the DDIM sampling trajectory of Stable Diffusion, leaving all model weights frozen.
  • On DrawBench (n=200): held-out memorability rose +3.42% (ResMem) and +2.72% (ViTMem), both p < 10⁻⁸, with no significant CLIP-fidelity loss; replicated on COCO (n=200) with equal or larger effect sizes.
  • Validated causality via reverse-guidance symmetry and a null random-critic control; characterized proxy over-optimization in SDXL and mapped a three-regime dose–response curve across an 8-point guidance-weight sweep.

First-Author Research · Manuscript
Mind the Gap: A Controlled Evaluation of Slice Separation and Masking in Self-Supervised CT Pretraining
Md. Ajmain Istiak Apon (First Author)
Research manuscript | Jan 2026 – Present
  • Built I-JEPA-CT, a 2.5D cross-slice joint-embedding predictive pretraining pipeline (ViT-B/16 context encoder, EMA target encoder, 4-layer Transformer predictor) trained on 1,082 unlabeled TotalSegmentator CT volumes for ~333k iterations on a single GPU.
  • Ran a controlled 2×2 factorial study over context–target slice gap and masking geometry across five label budgets; contrary to video-SSL intuition, narrowing the slice gap (A2) improved linear-probe Dice by 53–202% over the wide-gap baseline and dominated the masking factor, while combining both changes underperformed A2 by 12–17%, exposing a non-additive interaction.
  • Showed that the measured SSL benefit is protocol-dependent: A2 gains +102–118% over random init under linear probing but only +13.0 / +8.5 / +2.8% under end-to-end fine-tuning at N=20/50/100 (Welch t: p=0.017, p<0.001, p=0.054), with fine-tuning seed variance reduced 1.3–2.2×; A2 recovers 67% of supervised nnU-Net Dice at N=50 (liver 90%).
  • Tested robustness via cross-dataset transfer to AMOS22-CT (+158–260% over random init), CT→MR transfer with no MR pretraining (+41–48%), and a matched 3D SwinUNETR comparison that reproduces the same label-dependent decline in fine-tuning gain.
Stack: PyTorch, timm, MONAI, Nibabel, CUDA
